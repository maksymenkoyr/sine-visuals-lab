How Chladni picks a figure

The plate splits the sound into pitch slices, and the slice that is loudest compared with its own usual level puts its figure on the plate. So when the figure stays put, nothing new is happening in the music. The same slice keeps winning.

The rule, in three steps

  1. Cut the sound into pitch slices

    From the deepest bass to the highest treble. Each slice owns one figure. Low slices own big, simple figures; high slices own fine grids. You can see them above the bars.

  2. Each slice remembers its usual loudness

    Its average over the last 4 seconds. That is the thin line across each bar. A part that has been playing for a while has a high usual level. A part that just came in has a low one.

  3. Score = loud now − 80% of usual

    The slice with the highest score puts its figure on the plate. When a sound stops, its score fades out over a moment, so the plate rings a little.

Another way to read the score: a slice gets a small credit (20%) for how loud it usually is, plus full credit for anything above its usual right now. That is what “compared with its own recent average” means. The plate cares about what changed, not about what is loudest.

The melody note is quieter than the bass, yet it takes the plate, because it is new. Once it stops being new, the bass gets the plate back.

So a figure that stays means one of these

The same hit keeps coming

A kick on every beat jumps far above its usual each time, so its slice wins on every beat. The steady parts around it, bass and pads, only get their small credit.

One long note, and nothing new

A held note wins when it starts. Later it only keeps its small credit, but no other slice beats that, so its figure stays.

Silence

No slice scores anything, so the plate keeps the last figure. The sand stops moving too.

Figure hold is turned up

The figure on the plate gets a head start. A new one has to ring clearly stronger to take over, up to four times stronger at the top of the slider.

What makes it change

Something new in another slice: a melody, a vocal line, a chord change, a drop. Or a break: when the kick stops, the other slices finally get a turn.

What the figure does not tell you

  • How loud the music is. Loudness shows in how hard the sand bounces, not in which figure wins.
  • The tempo or the beat. A kick wins because each hit is new, not because it lands on the beat.
  • Which instrument. A kick and a low bass note in the same slice give the same figure.
  • An exact pitch. Pattern complexity is the plate's size. It slides every figure to a higher or lower slice, so the same note can give a different figure.
