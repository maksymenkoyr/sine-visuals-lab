How the Sand zones gauge works

The gauge shows one number: the plate's drive, how hard the plate is shaking right now.

The sand answers that number in three ways: it freezes, drifts to a new figure over seconds, or snaps onto it within a beat or two.

The two handles decide where those ranges start. The dots are a live preview of the sand at every drive.

The gaugeReading it

This is the same gauge as in the panel, running here on fake music. Drag either handle (or anywhere on the gauge to grab the nearer one).

drive 0.00 · sand freezes
  • White triangle: the drive right now.
  • Grey ticks: where it was over the last half second.
  • Purple handle: freeze edge. Below it the sand holds still.
  • Green handle: snap edge. Above it the sand jumps onto the figure.
  • Dots: grains, each one at a fixed drive, moved by the real sand rule.
  • Dashed line: a still line on the plate, where sand ends up.

The axis is squeezed toward the top end (a square-root scale) so the narrow freeze range near 0 stays wide enough to see and grab. It never rescales: 3 is always at the right end.

Step 1Where the drive number comes from

Every frame the scene adds two things: Vibration times how loud the music is, and Bass kick times the bass-hit pulse, which jumps up on each kick and falls back within a fraction of a second. Silence gives a small drive from Vibration alone, so a quiet plate barely moves.

intro

The trace is the last six seconds of the drive, on the same three ranges. In the drop the kicks poke up toward the snap edge; in the intro the drive sits low.

Step 2What the handles do to that number

The handles don't change the physics. The scene measured once where its sand freezes (drive 0.2) and where it snaps (1.5). Your handles say "treat my freeze edge as 0.2 and my snap edge as 1.5", and the scene stretches or squeezes the drive in between before the sand sees it. Above the snap edge it adds the rest one to one, so a harder shake still throws harder.

Across: the drive from the music. Up: the drive the sand actually feels. The dashed diagonal is "no change", which is exactly what the default handles give. The vertical lines are your handles and can be dragged here too.

Step 3Why the sand has three ranges

Each frame, every grain does two things. It hops a random distance, bigger where its spot of the plate shakes harder. And each hop leans a little toward the nearest still line. Below a small shake (the lift knee, 0.05) a grain only rattles in place and the lean fades to nothing, so the sand stays where it is. Higher up the lean adds up, slowly at first, then fast.

Measured where the plate shakes hardest, in nodal cells per second (a cell is the gap between two still lines). The grey curve is how far grains wander at random; the coloured one is how fast they are pulled toward the line. Below 0.2 both are almost zero. By 1.5 the pull is fast enough to bring half the sand in within a second.

MeasuredWhere 0.2 and 1.5 come from

The scene's sand rule, run on 3000 scattered grains over four different figures at a steady drive. The share of grains on the lines after one second and after four. Scattered sand already has about 16% on a line by chance (the white tick).

DriveOn the lines after 1 sAfter 4 s

Nothing happens below about 0.2, so the figure holds there. From 1.5, half the bed is on the lines within a second, about two beats at 124 BPM. In between the sand gets there, just slowly. The first explainer put the snap edge at 0.8; the real rule needs about twice that.
