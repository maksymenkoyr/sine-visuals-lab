Each idea has a live plate. They all run the scene's own sand rule on a fake 124 BPM song, so the sand moves the way it does in the app. The demos run on the CPU, so the sand is sparser than in the scene.

1New control

Finger on the plate

A plate can only ring in shapes that stay still where you hold it. Press a fingertip on it and every figure that would move under your finger dies away. Only the figures with a line running through that spot are left.

This is how Chladni chose his figures in 1787. He bowed the edge with one hand and held a finger on the plate with the other.

Tap or drag on the plate. The strip under it shows which figures your finger allows.

In the app
The music still picks the figure, but only from the ones your finger allows. You'd touch the plate on the laptop, or from the phone remote.
Work

2New card

Mode ladder

Each figure rings at its own note. When the music hits that note, the figure rings, and the plate draws the strongest few. Today all of this happens out of sight.

The ladder puts it on screen. There's one row per figure and a bar for how hard it rings right now, and the figures on the plate are marked. Click a figure to pin it. The plate then holds it through a quiet break or a whole drop, until you let go.

Click a figure to pin it, and click it again to let the music choose.

In the app
A Scene card fed by each mode's response from createPlateResponse. Pinning holds that one figure on the plate, whatever the music does.
Work
MediumA new card widget. The numbers it shows already exist.

3Look

Two-colour sand

The plate already sorts its sand by weight. Heavy grit bounces its way to the still lines. Fine powder is too light to bounce, and the air over the plate blows it into heaps between the lines. Faraday saw this in 1831.

Today grit and powder are drawn in the same colour, so the sorting is hard to see. Draw them in two colours from the palette and you watch the bed split in two.

Drag the divider. The left side is today, the right side is two colours.

In the app
A grain's colour comes from its weight, with grit in one palette ink and powder in another. The physics doesn't change.
Work
SmallThe grain shader already knows each grain's weight.

4Music moment

Toss the sand on the drop

Sand sitting on a line never moves, because the line is the part of the plate that stays still. So even a big kick does nothing to a figure that has formed. At a drop the old lines just slide into the new ones.

A toss throws the whole bed into the air the moment the drop lands. It comes down scattered, and over the next bar or two the new figure grows out of an even spread. The new figure takes about as long to form either way. The toss makes the drop something you can see in the sand.

Wait for the drop at bar 13, or press Drop now. Switch between Today and Toss.

In the app
A drop trigger, a jump in loudness after a quieter build, fires a single toss in the sand shader.
Work
SmallThe toss is simple. Most of the work is a drop trigger that doesn't fire on every loud bar.

5New figures

Other plates

Every figure the scene draws comes from one kind of plate: a square one with free edges. Change the plate and you get a whole new family of figures. A round plate makes rings and spokes, and a hexagon makes honeycombs. A guitar top makes the lopsided patterns luthiers use when they tune one. Clamping the square's edges gives it a new set of figures too.

Pick a plate.

In the app
A Plate setting, where each shape brings its own outline and its own table of figures.
