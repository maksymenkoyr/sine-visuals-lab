Why fast hits kept pulling the rings back

Each hit starts a ring in a fixed pool. The old pool reused its oldest ring on every hit, even when that ring was still mid-screen. Push the hit rate up and watch the left side.

Shipped in #154

1 · The ring pool, before and after

Both sides get the same hits. A ring needs about 2.8 s to travel from the centre to the corner of a 16:9 frame (radius ≈ 3.06 at 1.1 units/s). The old pool had 8 rings, so above roughly 3 hits/s it recycled rings before they got there. Turn the driver off and the old side "lets go": the last rings are finally left alone and run out.

2 · Why some sources do nothing

Beat ripple asks drives.fired() "did a hit happen this frame?". Only hit-type sources can answer that.

Hit sources

Beat, Bass hit, Mid hit, High hit, Drop, beat-grid stops. They have a one-shot edge (kind: "edge" in signals.ts), so each hit starts one ring.

Level sources and drawn lines

Loudness, band levels, a frequency line. They have no edge, so sourceEdge() silently returns the scene's own default trigger.

What you see

You patch a level into Beat ripple, the picker accepts it, and the rings still follow bass hits. Nothing tells you the choice was ignored.

3 · One converter for every kind of signal

Fire from each source's 0–1 value instead of from a hard-coded edge. It fires when the value rises past the upper mark, and can't fire again until the value falls below the lower mark. There is a minimum gap between fires, and while a loud signal is held, it re-fires on the beat. Pick a signal and compare the three rows of fire ticks.

signal value upper / lower marks Today: default hits (your signal ignored unless it is a hit source) Plain threshold: chatters on noise Proposed: hysteresis + gap + held re-fire

The marks here are fixed at 0.6 / 0.4 for the demo. The open question is whether they should be draggable per setting, like the silence-gate marks, or adapt to each source's recent range the way auto-gain does.
